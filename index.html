Training

How to fight

The sound check is further down this page. Each enemy button lists its health and what to expect.

Where enemies are. Each enemy makes its own sound, panned left or right. Sounds behind you are muffled and dull. Louder means closer.

Aiming. Turn left and turn right rotate you 15 degrees. About face spins you all the way around. When an enemy is in your sights you hear a fast high ping. Closer enemies ping higher. The ping sounds the same on every map, and the background ambience dips while something is in your sights so the ping stays clear. You can raise it with the Lock-on ping volume slider in Settings. Facing north plays a bright two-note chime.

Range. Battlefields are big. Enemies appear close, at mid-range, or far off, up to about 160 feet away, and every enemy is announced with its distance in feet. Far-off enemies sound quieter and duller. Pistols and submachine guns are only fully effective to about 40 feet, and rifles to about 85 to 100 feet; past that they lose damage and accuracy. Shotguns lose power past about 23 feet, and their blast also hurts enemies standing within 10 feet of the one you hit. Hollow point rounds hit much harder within 25 feet but weaker far away. Sniper rifles never lose damage, so they are the tool for long shots. Any round that punches through enemies, like the anti-tank rifle, the Jet rifle, or the M249 with armor piercing rounds, never loses damage to distance either. Attacking enemies are about 4 feet from you.

Headshots. When the lock-on ping turns into a double chirp, the enemy is dead centre. Shots then land as headshots for 60 percent more damage, 30 percent on bosses, with a crisp crack on impact. A reflex sight makes headshots hit much harder. A sniper scope turns most long-range hits into headshots. With both Ramjet ammo upgrades on, a Jet rifle headshot hits for 10,000. A precision scope makes sniper headshots hit far harder and almost every sniper shot land. A grip widens the dead-centre zone and turns some off-centre hits into headshots. Shotguns cannot headshot unless they have a grip.

Enemy locations reads out every enemy by clock position, distance in feet and health percent. 12 o'clock is straight ahead, 3 o'clock is to your right, 6 o'clock is behind you. After you wound an enemy you also hear its health percent.

Hits and misses. Every bullet that lands makes a short thud from where the enemy is: a soft thud on people and wolves, a metallic tink on drones and mechs, a deep heavy thud on armored brutes, raiders and bosses. A bullet zipping past the enemy means you missed it. A distant ricochet or dirt puff means nothing was there.

Reloading. Guns reload by themselves the moment the magazine runs dry, and when you switch to an empty gun. Press Reload any time to top up early.

Holding fire. Tap Fire for one trigger pull. To keep firing, press and hold the Fire button. With VoiceOver, double-tap and hold. On a keyboard, hold Space. A faint tick on each shot means your magazine is running low.

Cover. Take cover halves the damage you take, but your aim gets shakier and the battle sounds muffled. Leave cover to aim steady again. Bosses roar as they wind up a big attack: that is your cue to take cover. The Iron Revenant fires rockets from range, so listen for the whistle.

Grenades go off around you, so there is no aiming. Frag grenades blast every enemy within 5 feet, perfect when enemies are attacking you. Cryo grenades freeze every enemy within 8 feet to about a third of their speed for 8 seconds. The Fat Man destroys everything within 50 feet instantly, bosses included, after a falling whistle. A jack-in-the-box is a proximity bomb: it sits 10 feet in front of you playing a little music-box tune, and when an enemy comes within 7 feet of it, the lid pops with a boing and it explodes, hitting everything within 15 feet of the box. None of them hurt you. Grenade type cycles between all four.

Modes. Campaign is five waves ending in the battlefield's boss. Endless survival never stops, brings a boss every fifth wave and a mega boss with twice a boss's health every seventh wave, and pays a bonus for every wave you clear. Boss rush is only bosses: it starts with one weakened boss and gets brutal far faster than endless, with a mega boss every seventh wave. Practice range has still targets that cannot hurt you, for learning a gun. Zombie siege is endless waves of the undead. Four glass windows surround you about 50 feet out, at north, east, south and west. A zombie has to smash through a window to reach you, which takes a few seconds: you hear banging and cracking glass from that direction, then a crash when it shatters. Broken windows seal themselves again about 6 seconds later. Every tenth wave brings a giant zombie boss. The horde grows every wave, to about 200 zombies by wave 50. Zombies hit softer than soldiers and pay 20 percent less silver, but kills pay 8 percent more every wave. Status reads the state of every window. That is the Standard zombie mode.

Watch my six. A zombie mode with only two windows: one straight ahead of you at 12 o'clock and one right behind you at 6 o'clock. Nothing comes from the left or right, so About face is your best friend.

Horror hallway. A zombie mode where you walk down a long hallway on your own, with no walk button. Your footsteps tell you your pace: quick, close together steps when the hallway ahead is clear, slowing down the more zombies are ahead, until you stop completely. You also stop while a zombie is right in front of you or grabbing you. Zombies break out of wooden barriers and glass windows in the walls ahead: you hear wood thudding and splintering, or glass banging and cracking, then the crash as they get through. If you walk past one, it chases you from behind, fast. A soft double tick plays every 100 feet. Your score is every foot you walk plus 10 points for every kill. Status reads your distance, kills and score.

Perk machine. In every zombie mode, after you clear every fifth wave the perk machine opens with a music-box jingle. Perks cost silver and last for the rest of that run. Electric fence: an electrified band from 20 feet in to 14 feet around you. A zombie touching it makes a loud electric crack, then sharp zaps while it struggles through at 60 percent speed, taking damage every second. Weaker zombies usually fry, but tougher zombies in later waves get through hurt, and runners can dash across. Giant zombies take more damage but are not slowed. A soft hum every few seconds tells you the fence is on. Iron gut: 50 more maximum health. Quick hands: reloads twice as fast. Double tap: your shots hit 35 percent harder. Second wind: the first time you would fall, you get back up with half your health.

Mythical weapons. Thunderclap chains lightning: listen for crackles jumping between enemies. Thunderclap upgrades: a static field makes shocked enemies buzz and move at half speed, the ball lightning slug fires a fizzing orb straight ahead that passes through up to 7 enemies, scorches anyone within 2 feet of them and bursts into chain lightning, and when the lightning rod is charged you hear a rising hum and then electricity crackling around you until you fire your supercharged blast with its long deep rumble. The Black Hole Cannon's orb hums where it lands, drags enemies toward it, then collapses with a boom. The Banshee's scream hits and stuns everything in front of you, and stunned enemies make a ringing sound. It is strongest within 40 feet and weakens out to 160 feet. The Hydra's three rockets each explode where their target is, so you hear up to three blasts in different places. The Alien Symbiote fires wet, fleshy bursts. It only feeds when you use it: every kill it makes eats 2 of your maximum health and makes it stronger, at 40 percent more damage for every 50 health eaten. Now and then you hear it feed, a squelch, a growl and a heartbeat, and you hear its damage each time it passes another whole multiple. It is fully grown at 600 damage per second, or 2400 with Feeding frenzy, or when your maximum health is down to 100. Status tells you how strong it has grown. Carrying it without using it never costs you health. With vampiric rounds, every kill it makes gives you back 2 health with a soft heartbeat pulse. With Hair trigger V2, the Banshee becomes a tactical rifle: each trigger pull is a tight burst of 3 quick shrieks that hit harder, with a short pause between bursts. The Jack Frost chills hard: each icicle slows an enemy more, down to a tenth of its speed after four hits. Its Blizzard blast goes off when you reload: a whirling wind storm circles you, then a crunch of ice as every enemy within 40 feet freezes completely still for 15 seconds. Ice cracking means they are moving again, and a crystal chime means the next blizzard is ready, 10 seconds after the last. The Jet rifle's heat vent goes off with a roaring rush of hot air, bigger the more rounds you fired. With both Ramjet upgrades, a Jet round punches through every enemy in its line, with no limit, and never skips one, even from cover.

Silver and the Armory. Every kill pays silver: 10 for light enemies, 16 for heavy ones, 50 for mini bosses, 83 for bosses and 250 for mega bosses. In endless survival, boss rush and zombie siege, every kill pays 8 percent more silver for each wave you have survived, so wave 11 pays almost double. Every regular wave has one mini boss: it announces itself with a rising two-note siren before its cry, and it is far tougher than a normal enemy. Spend it in the Armory shop on weapons, attachments, body armor, grenades and perks. Perks go up to level 50. Kills also raise each gun's mastery, up to level 10, making its attachments stronger.

Keyboard. Left and right arrows turn. Down arrow is about face. Hold Space to fire. R reloads. Q switches weapon. C takes cover. G throws a grenade. X switches grenade type. E reads enemy locations. T is status. Escape pauses.
